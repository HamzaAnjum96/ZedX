Agile Sprints

Agile Sprints is for teams that deliver software, technology projects and other work in Scrum. Stories wait in a prioritised backlog, move across a board during each sprint and go into planned releases.

Plan from a prioritised backlog

Epics hold the bigger requirements, and user stories break them into the work that delivers them. Both sit in a prioritised product backlog.

At sprint planning, the team estimates stories in story points and takes on only what its capacity allows. Everyone sees the scope and the sprint goal before work starts.

The demo backlog has three stages of its own: Ideas & Intake, Refinement Needed and Ready for Sprint.

See how epics overlap

Each epic has an owner, a priority and delivery dates. A Gantt chart lays the epics out week by week, so the team can see how the bigger pieces of work overlap across sprints. Releases can span several sprints, and each story can be assigned to a planned release version.

Run the sprint, then review it

Sprint board
Each sprint has its own board, with stages you can configure. It shows what is planned, in progress, blocked or finished.
Board views
The side menu switches between Backlog, Current Sprint, Planned Sprints, Historic Sprints, Epics and Archived.
Stand-ups and reviews
Agile Sprints supports daily stand-ups and sprint reviews, and stand-up reporting is automated. Sprint progress updates in real time.
Timeboxed sprints
Each sprint has a velocity target to compare with the actual result. When the sprint is over, the team formally closes it.
Metrics
Completion, velocity and carried-over work for each sprint, with trends across sprints. The official page presents these figures as a way for teams to learn and to estimate better. Insights dashboards show which workstreams need a manager’s attention.
Access
Role-based permissions decide who can do what. People can also be given view-only access to a workstream.

Questions about Agile Sprints

Is it only for software teams?

No. It’s for teams that deliver software, technology projects or other work in Scrum or another sprint-based method.

Can several teams use it on different projects?

Yes. A project can hold several workstreams, each an Agile board with its own epics, sprints and team. Each team runs its own backlog and sprints. Portfolios group related projects, so you can oversee several at once.

Does it track velocity?

Yes. Each sprint has a velocity target, and Agile Sprints records the actual velocity, the work completed and anything carried into the next sprint. Trends across sprints help the team estimate more accurately.

Does Agile Sprints include AI?

Yes. Agile Sprints includes Ask Cloe, the AI assistant built into ZedX. You can ask it questions and get guidance on Agile and Scrum work. Swiftpro says Cloe uses OpenAI technology.

Which editions include it?

Agile Sprints is currently part of the Professional and Group editions. The Home edition doesn’t include it. In Corporate, Enterprise and Enterprise Plus you can choose any ZedX app, Agile Sprints included. Compare the editions

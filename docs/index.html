Group WorkStreams takes everyday work out of spreadsheets

Complaints, catering faults, HR requests and finance queries can each become a workstream, with its own Kanban board, stages and team. Everyone works from one board instead of a spreadsheet.

  • Each service has its own boardStages are set for each workstream. In the demo, the complaints log has five and catering issues have three.
  • Ownership you can seeEach work item shows its priority, from Low to Critical, and who has it once it is assigned.
  • Managers see the workloadA dashboard shows where work is building up, without asking each person for an update.
See how Group WorkStreams works

Agile Sprints takes Scrum teams from backlog to release

Epics and user stories wait in a prioritised backlog, move across a board during each sprint and go into planned releases.

See how Agile Sprints works
  • Sprints start with a realistic goalThe team estimates stories in story points and takes on only what its capacity allows.
  • Standup reporting is automatedThe sprint board shows what is in progress, finished or blocked.
  • Velocity is measured every sprintThe team checks velocity against its target and any work carried over, then formally closes the sprint.

Everyday requests move across boards of their own. Stories move from backlog to sprint to release. Both apps share one ZedX login.

How the work moves, in three steps

Choose an app to follow its work from the first item to the result.

Group WorkStreams

  1. 1

    Give each service a workstream

    Complaints, catering faults or HR requests each get their own workstream, team and owners, grouped by department.

  2. 2

    Set the stages, then log the work

    Add stages on the board with New Stage and Edit Stage, then add a work item whenever a request comes in.

  3. 3

    Watch the workload

    The Insights dashboard counts the work items in every workstream you can access and shows which are critical.

Agile Sprints

  1. 1

    Fill and rank the backlog

    Epics hold the bigger requirements, and user stories break them into work, in priority order.

  2. 2

    Plan the sprint to capacity

    Estimate stories in story points and take on what the team’s capacity allows, with the sprint goal in view.

  3. 3

    Run it, review it, close it

    Work from the sprint board, check velocity against the target and any work carried over, then close the sprint.

Group WorkStreams and Agile Sprints compared
Group WorkStreams Agile Sprints
Who it suits Operational, administrative, service and departmental teams with a steady flow of requests and cases. Teams delivering software, technology projects or other work in Scrum or another method based on sprints.
How work arrives At any time, as requests, cases, issues and routine jobs. As epics and user stories in a prioritised backlog, planned before each sprint.
How it is planned There is no fixed cycle. Work items move through the workstream’s stages as people handle them. In timeboxed sprints. The team estimates stories in story points and fills each sprint up to its capacity.
Board views Backlog, Current Stages, Epic and Archived. A workstream can also be shown as a simple list or a timeline. Backlog, Current Sprint, Planned Sprints, Historic Sprints, Epics and Archived.
How progress is reviewed On dashboards of workload, progress and bottlenecks across workstreams, including how many items are critical. In daily standups and sprint reviews, using completion, velocity and work carried over.

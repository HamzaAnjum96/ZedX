Two ZedX apps for running work

Workflows for everyday work. Sprints for planned delivery.

Group WorkStreams handles the steady flow of operational work, with a Kanban board for each function. Agile Sprints plans and runs Scrum delivery, from backlog to sprint to release.

Group WorkStreams

Continuous work, organised by function

Set up a workstream for each function, such as complaints, catering, HR requests or finance queries. Its tasks sit on a Kanban board, with stages that fit that kind of work.

More about Group WorkStreams
Suits
Operational teams handling a steady flow of requests, cases and issues: continuous-flow, ad-hoc and non-Scrum work.
How work is organised
Each workstream has its own board and its own stages. In the demo, the complaints log runs through five stages; catering issues need three.
What teams can see
Each workstream’s portfolio, active epics and active tasks, plus a dashboard counting work items per workstream, split into critical and non-critical.

Agile Sprints

Planned delivery, sprint by sprint

For teams working in Scrum. Epics and user stories sit in a prioritised backlog, are estimated in story points and are planned into sprints against the team’s capacity.

More about Agile Sprints
Suits
Delivery teams working in Scrum or sprint-based Agile, where work is planned before it starts.
How work is organised
Stories move from the backlog onto a configurable sprint board. Releases can span one or more sprints, with stories assigned to planned versions.
What teams can see
Support for daily stand-ups and sprint reviews, online automated stand-up reporting, and metrics for sprint completion, velocity and carried-over work.

Continuous workflow or sprints?

Both apps put work on boards. The difference is how the work arrives and how it is planned.

Group WorkStreams and Agile Sprints compared
Group WorkStreams Agile Sprints
Incoming work Arrives continuously: requests, cases, issues and jobs. Planned in advance as epics and user stories in a prioritised backlog.
Planning cadence No fixed cycle. Items move through their workstream’s stages as they are handled. Sprint by sprint. Stories are estimated in story points and selected against team capacity.
The board One board per workstream, with stages set for that function. A board for the current sprint, with configurable workflow stages.
Reviewing progress Active tasks per workstream, and a dashboard of work items split into critical and non-critical. Daily stand-ups and sprint reviews, with sprint completion, velocity and carried-over work.

Running both? The official Agile Sprints page suggests using Group WorkStreams alongside it when teams mix delivery models, so operational and ad-hoc work stays out of the sprint.

What leadership sees: periodic updates, not task lists

The boards stay with the teams. Leaders follow delivery in a third ZedX app, Project Portfolios, which collects structured updates from project managers at agreed intervals, such as monthly or quarterly.

Every update uses the same format: delivery confidence as a RAG status, narrative progress, key achievements, blockers, risks, dependencies and support required. Dashboards show delivery health across portfolios, programmes and projects, and trends show whether confidence is improving, stable or deteriorating.

Project workstreams can be linked to Group WorkStreams or to Agile Sprints. Their progress then reaches Project Portfolios through those periodic updates, not as task-level detail.

Project Portfolios also covers vision and objectives, a portfolio-level Gantt view, export to Excel, automatic reminders for updates and tracking benefits after delivery. Project Portfolios on zedxapps.com
